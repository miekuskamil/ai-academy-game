# Neuron — the real-world AI atlas

The complaint was that the content felt machine-driven, not human. This atlas is
the fix at the root: a catalogue of AI as it actually shows up in a kid's real
life, so every practical lesson can be anchored to something she has genuinely
seen. Each entry is written plainly enough to drop straight into a lesson.

For each application: **in** (what it gets) → **does** (what it does) → **out**
(what it produces) → **human check** (what a person still has to do) → **watch
out** (what goes wrong) → **try** (a safe thing she can do).

The safety rule runs through all of it: **never upload private faces, documents,
addresses, medical info, passwords, or school records to public AI.** Use made-up
or supplied examples. This is stated in lessons, not just here.

---

## Everyday helpers she has already met

**Chatbots (answering questions, writing help)**
- in: your typed question · does: predicts a helpful answer · out: text
- human check: is it actually true? did it do what I asked?
- watch out: sounds confident even when wrong (makes things up)
- try: ask the same thing two ways, see how the answer changes

**Voice assistants (Alexa, Siri, "Hey Google")**
- in: your spoken words · does: turns speech to text, then answers · out: an action or a spoken reply
- human check: did it hear the right words?
- watch out: mishears, or does the wrong thing confidently
- try: say the same request three ways, see which it gets right

**Translation (Google Translate)**
- in: text in one language · does: maps it to another · out: translated text
- human check: does it actually mean the same thing?
- watch out: idioms and jokes break; formal/casual tone is lost
- try: translate a sentence to another language and back — see what changed

**Search engines**
- in: your search words · does: ranks pages, now also writes a summary · out: links + an AI summary
- human check: is the summary right? click through and see
- watch out: the summary can be wrong while sounding official
- try: check an AI search summary against the actual page

## Seeing and reading

**Object detection in a camera (Google Lens, phone camera)**
- in: a photo · does: finds and names objects · out: labels/boxes
- human check: did it name them right?
- watch out: odd angles, bad light, things it never learned
- try: point it at ten things, count how many it gets right

**Scanning text from a photo (OCR — receipts, worksheets)**
- in: a photo of text · does: turns the picture into typed text · out: editable text
- human check: read it back — numbers especially
- watch out: messy handwriting, smudges, 0 vs O
- try: scan a printed page (not private), check for mistakes

**Face detection vs face recognition** *(the responsibility one)*
- detection asks "is there a face?" · recognition asks "*whose* face is it?"
- human check: recognition is often wrong, and people should be told it's used
- watch out: a face is sensitive personal info; never collect someone's without asking
- try: use cartoon/drawn faces only; talk about when it would feel unfair

## Choosing and predicting

**Recommendations (YouTube, games, shops)**
- in: what you watched/played/bought · does: finds patterns, guesses what's next · out: a "you might like" list
- human check: is this actually good, or just similar?
- watch out: keeps you in a bubble; nudges you to keep scrolling
- try: notice *why* something was recommended to you

**Maps and traffic (Google Maps)**
- in: your start + end, live traffic · does: predicts the fastest route · out: directions + a time
- human check: does the route make sense in real life?
- watch out: sends people down silly roads; predictions are guesses
- try: compare its time estimate to how long it really took

**Spam and fraud detection**
- in: an email/payment · does: scores how dodgy it looks · out: "spam" / "allowed" / "blocked"
- human check: real things get blocked, scams get through — check the folder
- watch out: false alarms and misses both happen
- try: look in a spam folder, spot a real one caught by mistake

## Making things

**Image generators**
- in: a text prompt · does: builds an image from patterns · out: a picture
- human check: hands, text, and counts are often wrong
- watch out: can copy styles; can make convincing fakes
- try: prompt the same idea three ways, see how much the words matter

**Turning data into a spreadsheet**
- in: a list (e.g. made-up expenses) · does: sorts into rows/columns, adds it up · out: a table
- human check: do the totals add up? right columns?
- watch out: miscategorises, or invents a number
- try: give it a made-up shopping list, ask for a tidy table, check the maths

## Big serious ones (talk about, don't operate)

**Medical image helpers** — a doctor's tool that flags a *possible* finding; a
clinician always decides. Good example of "AI is one step, a human owns the call."

**Factory & warehouse robots** — cameras + arms sorting and moving things; humans
set the rules and handle exceptions.

**Weather & climate prediction** — huge pattern models over massive data; still
probabilities, not certainty.

## How this maps to the 20 lessons

Every practical lesson gets a real anchor from this atlas, so nothing feels
abstract:

- **W1 Talking to AI** → chatbots, image generators (prompting changes the output you can *see*)
- **W3 Using AI for real** → search summaries (trust/check), recommendations (when not to), maps (a thinking partner that can be wrong)
- **W3/W4 judgment & bias** → face recognition (the responsibility lesson), spam (false alarms), recommendations (bubbles)
- **W5 Building** → data→spreadsheet, vibe-coding an app, an AI helper reading a sensor on a Pi
- **W6 Agents** → maps/route-planning and warehouse robots as "a goal + tools + steps"

## The safety spine (repeated in-app, not buried here)

- Never upload private faces, documents, addresses, medical info, passwords, or school records to public AI.
- Use made-up or provided examples for every hands-on activity.
- Face data and personal documents need a grown-up and a good reason.
- AI answers get checked, always — especially confident ones.
